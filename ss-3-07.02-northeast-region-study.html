Where Is the Northeast?
📖
Land, Water, and Weather of the Northeast
A walk through the region before you map it
📖
The Northeast Alcove Opens

The Northeast is in the upper-right corner of the United States, along the Atlantic Ocean. It includes: Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland.

🏔️ The land. The Appalachian Mountains run along the western edge, stretching from Maine down through Pennsylvania. East of the mountains, the land flattens toward the coast. Harbors and inlets made the Northeast a natural place for fishing villages and trading ports.

🌊 The water. The Atlantic Ocean borders the entire eastern side. The Hudson River (New York) and the Delaware River are important waterways. Niagara Falls sits on the western edge near the Great Lakes.

❄️ The climate. The Northeast has four distinct seasons. Winters can be very cold and snowy — especially in New England (Maine, Vermont, New Hampshire). Summers are warm. Hurricanes occasionally reach the coast.

🦞 Resources. Fishing (especially lobster and cod off the New England coast), maple syrup from Vermont and New Hampshire, coal from Pennsylvania (historically very important), and some of the oldest seaports in the country.

🏙️ Cities. Boston, New York City, Philadelphia, and Baltimore are some of the oldest and largest cities in the country. They grew up near harbors because trade and shipping were easy.

🤔

Pause & Think: If you visited the Northeast, would you head for the rocky coastline of Maine, the Appalachian Mountains in Vermont, or the streets of New York City? Why?

The Big Picture
🏷️ Exhibit Label

The Northeast region sits in the upper-right corner of the United States, along the Atlantic Ocean. It has the Appalachian Mountains, four clear seasons, and some of the oldest cities in the country. From fishing villages in Maine to the skyscrapers of New York City, the Northeast is shaped by its coastline, its mountains, and its long history.

🦉
🧭 Opti says…
The Appalachian Mountains are some of the oldest mountains on Earth — much older than the Rockies out West! They've been worn down by millions of years of wind and rain. Back in the time of the dinosaurs, they were probably as tall as the Rockies are today!
