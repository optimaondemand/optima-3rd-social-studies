Where Is the West?
📖
Land, Water, and Weather of the West
A walk through the biggest region before you map it

The West is the biggest region in the United States. It includes the whole left side of the country plus two states that don't even touch the rest: Alaska way up north and Hawaii way out in the Pacific Ocean. The other West states are California, Oregon, Washington, Idaho, Montana, Wyoming, Nevada, Utah, and Colorado.

🏔️ The land. The tall, snowy Rocky Mountains run north to south through the heart of the West. Closer to the Pacific are the Sierra Nevada and the Cascade Range. The Cascades have active volcanoes! Mount St. Helens in Washington erupted in 1980. Other features include the Mojave Desert in California, the Great Salt Lake in Utah, and the giant redwood forests on the California coast. Alaska has icy tundra and huge glaciers, and it is the biggest state of all: more than twice the size of Texas. Hawaii, about 2,500 miles from California, has active volcanoes and tropical beaches. The West is not all desert!

🌊 The water. The Pacific Ocean stretches along the entire west coast. The Colorado River starts high in the Rocky Mountains of Colorado and flows southwest, toward the Grand Canyon. Alaska has water on three sides, with the Pacific to the south and the Arctic Ocean to the north.

🌤️ The weather. The West has the most different weather of any region. Mountain peaks have snow all year. The Mojave Desert can hit 120°F. Seattle and Portland get many days of gentle rain, especially in fall and winter. Alaska winters are long and dark. Hawaii is warm and tropical year-round.

🦅 The people and the work. The West is home to many groups: Native American nations such as the Tlingit and Iñupiat in Alaska, the Nez Perce in Idaho, and the Shoshone; Native Hawaiians, whose ancestors sailed to the islands about 1,000 years ago; descendants of Chinese workers who helped build the railroads in the 1860s; Mexican-American families across California and the Southwest border; and many newer arrivals. Big cities include Los Angeles, San Francisco, Seattle, and Denver. Work includes growing fruit and almonds in California, fishing in Alaska, making movies in Hollywood, building computers in Silicon Valley, and welcoming visitors to Yellowstone and Yosemite National Parks. The West is much more than California and Hollywood: it has eleven states, from Montana ranches to Alaska fishing towns.

⏳

Then & Now: Long ago, Native peoples of the Pacific Northwest carved giant cedar logs into longhouses, canoes, and totem poles, using only stone tools and skills passed down across generations. Today, many Native nations still carve those same forms, sometimes alongside modern artists. What stayed the same about why the great trees of the West matter?
