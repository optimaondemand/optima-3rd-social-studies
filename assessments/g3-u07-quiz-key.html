1Pick all that are true · 1 point · 3.07.01 Five Regions of the United States

Geographers group states into regions. Which of these do they look at? Pick ALL that are true.

  • A How many letters are in each state's name
  • B The land and the climate the states share ✔
  • C The resources the states share ✔
  • D The ways people live there ✔

Why: A region is a group of states that are alike in their land, weather (climate), resources, and ways of life. The length of a state's name has nothing to do with it.

2Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.07.01 Five Regions of the United States

Which list names the five regions of the United States?

  • A North, South, East, West, and Middle
  • B Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, and West ✔
  • C Canada, United States, Mexico, Caribbean, and Alaska
  • D Atlantic, Pacific, Gulf of Mexico, Great Lakes, and Rio Grande

Why: Many maps divide the United States into five regions: the Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, and West.

3Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.07.02 Northeast Region Study

In early spring in Vermont, people collect sap from maple trees and boil it. What do they make?

  • A Coal
  • B Cotton
  • C Lobster traps
  • D Maple syrup ✔

Why: Cold nights and warm days in early spring make the sap run. People boil the sap into maple syrup. Vermont makes more maple syrup than any other state.

4Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.07.03 Southeast Region Study

Hurricanes often hit the Southeast. Where does a hurricane get its strength?

  • A From warm ocean water ✔
  • B From snow on the mountains
  • C From dry desert wind
  • D From the cold Great Lakes

Why: A hurricane forms over warm ocean water, and the warm water feeds it. When it moves over land, it has no warm water to feed it, so it gets weaker.

5Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.07.04 Midwest Region Study

Why are the Great Lakes so important to the Midwest?

  • A They are salty oceans full of whales.
  • B They are mountains covered in snow.
  • C They hold fresh water that millions of people drink. ✔
  • D They are dry lakes in the desert.

Why: The Great Lakes are five large freshwater lakes. They hold about one fifth of all the fresh water on top of the Earth, and millions of people drink it.

6Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.07.05 Southwest Region Study

What carved the Grand Canyon in Arizona?

  • A An earthquake that happened in one day
  • B The Colorado River, a little at a time, over millions of years ✔
  • C People with big digging machines
  • D A glacier made of ice

Why: The Colorado River carved the Grand Canyon a little at a time, over millions of years. Now it is more than a mile deep.

7Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.07.05 Southwest Region Study

Many old houses in the Southwest desert have thick adobe walls. How do thick walls help?

  • A They make the house taller.
  • B They keep the rain out, because it rains every day.
  • C They keep snow off the roof all winter.
  • D They keep the house cooler at noon and warmer at night. ✔

Why: Thick adobe walls soak up the heat slowly all day and give it back slowly at night. The house stays cooler at noon and warmer at midnight. Adobe is a brick made of mud and straw, dried in the sun.

8True / false · 1 point · 3.07.06 West Region Study

True or false: Hawaii is a U.S. state in the West region, made of islands that volcanoes built.

  • T True ✔
  • F False

Why: Hawaii is a West state made of volcanic islands in the Pacific Ocean. Lava from volcanoes like Kīlauea is still building new land.

9Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.07.07 Songs, Legends, Poems, and News

Paul Bunyan, the giant logger, comes from a tall tale. What is a tall tale?

  • A A true news story from a newspaper
  • B A map that shows where forests are
  • C A funny legend that stretches the truth ✔
  • D A poem about a real president

Why: A tall tale is a funny legend that stretches the truth. Paul Bunyan was never a real person, but his story tells us about a region with huge forests and logging camps.

10Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.07.08 Contributions from Diverse Groups

Sequoyah made a writing system for the Cherokee language. In his writing, what does each symbol stand for?

  • A A whole sound, like tsa or la ✔
  • B A whole sentence
  • C A number
  • D A picture of an animal

Why: Sequoyah's writing system is a syllabary: each symbol stands for one whole sound. Three symbols, tsa-la-gi, spell Cherokee. Soon many Cherokee people could read and write in their own language.
