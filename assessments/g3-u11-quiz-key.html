Which is the strongest label for a map slide?

  • A "Map."
  • B "This is a very nice map."
  • C "This map shows the Northeast, where many big cities and old harbors are." ✔
  • D A long list of every fact about the Northeast, with big, fancy words.

3True or false · 1 point · 3.11.02 Build the Final Community-in-the-World Exhibit

True or false: More words always make a slide better.

  • True
  • False ✔

Why: False. Courtesy for a visitor means short, clear words and a real picture. Too many words make a visitor walk away.

5Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.11.03 Present the Evidence: Map, Source, and Virtue

A visitor asks you a question, and you don't know the answer. What does an honest guide say?

  • A "Good question. I'm not sure, but here is what I do know." ✔
  • B Make up an answer that sounds smart.
  • C Pretend you didn't hear the question.
  • D "I know everything about this place."

Why: An honest guide says "I'm not sure" and then shares what they really know. Making things up is not honest.

6Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.11.03 Present the Evidence: Map, Source, and Virtue

A real photo shows a farmer and his sons walking through a dust storm in 1936. Which sentence is honest?

  • A "This photo proves the Great Plains is always dusty."
  • B "This photo proves every farm had a dust storm."
  • C "This photo shows a farmer and his sons walking through a dust storm in 1936." ✔
  • D "This is the best photo anyone ever took."

Why: A source shows some things, not everything. An honest guide says what the photo shows and does not stretch it.
