1True / false · 1 point · 3.06.01 Canada, U.S., Mexico, Caribbean

True or false: The Caribbean is one big country south of Florida.

  • T True
  • F False ✔

Why: The Caribbean is not one country. It is a group of islands in a warm sea, south of Florida and east of Mexico.

2Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.06.02 Physical Features

Which mountain range runs down the western side of Canada and the United States?

  • A The Appalachian Mountains
  • B The Sierra Madre
  • C The Rocky Mountains ✔
  • D The Great Plains

Why: The Rocky Mountains run down the western side. The Appalachians are on the eastern side, the Sierra Madre is in Mexico, and the Great Plains are flat land, not mountains.

3Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.06.03 Landmarks

Which landmark is a natural landmark, made by nature and not built by people?

  • A The Grand Canyon ✔
  • B The Statue of Liberty
  • C The CN Tower
  • D The pyramid at Chichén Itzá

Why: The Grand Canyon was carved by the Colorado River over millions of years. The other three were built by people.

4Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.06.04 Climate and Vegetation

A saguaro cactus stores water inside its thick stem. Which climate zone does it grow in?

  • A The Arctic tundra in far northern Canada
  • B The tropical Caribbean islands
  • C The boreal forest across Canada
  • D The desert in the southwestern United States and northern Mexico ✔

Why: The desert is very hot and very dry. The saguaro can live there because it stores water in its stem.

5Pick all that are true · 1 point · 3.06.05 Natural Resources

Which of these are renewable resources that can grow back? Pick ALL that are true.

  • A Trees (timber) ✔
  • B Wheat ✔
  • C Oil
  • D Copper

Why: Trees grow back if new ones are planted, and wheat is planted and harvested every year. Oil and copper formed over millions of years and cannot be made again.

6Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.06.05 Natural Resources

Why is oil called a nonrenewable resource?

  • A It grows back every year if farmers plant it.
  • B Rain and snow refill it every spring.
  • C It took millions of years to form, and once it is used up, it is gone for a very long time. ✔
  • D It is only found on the Caribbean islands.

Why: Oil formed deep in the Earth over millions of years. People can pump it up, but no one can make more.

7Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.06.06 Cultures That Settled

Which people built stone cities with stepped pyramids, and were one of the first peoples in the world to use a sign for zero?

  • A The Haudenosaunee
  • B The Maya ✔
  • C French settlers
  • D Spanish settlers

Why: Over 1,000 years ago, the Maya built stone cities with stepped pyramids, like Chichén Itzá. Millions of Maya people still live in Mexico, Guatemala, and Belize today.

8Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.06.07 Environment and Settlement

Why did many big cities, like New Orleans and Havana, grow next to a river or a harbor?

  • A Boats could carry goods in and out, so the water meant trade. ✔
  • B People wanted to live close to a place to swim.
  • C Rivers and harbors always have the best weather.
  • D Cities next to water never have storms.

Why: A river or a harbor lets boats bring goods in and carry goods out. That is trade.

9Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.06.07 Environment and Settlement

Why did Denver, Colorado, grow into a big city?

  • A It has a deep ocean harbor for big ships.
  • B It was built on an island in a lake.
  • C The land near Denver is great for growing bananas.
  • D Gold was found in the nearby Rocky Mountains. ✔

Why: Gold was found near Denver in 1858, and miners rushed in. Gold is something valuable, a resource.

10Multiple choice · 1 point · 3.06.08 Comparing Cultures

Baseball is loved in the United States, Mexico, Cuba, and the Dominican Republic. What does this show?

  • A Every place in the region now has the same culture.
  • B Only the United States plays sports.
  • C Cultures can travel between places, and each place still has its own culture. ✔
  • D Nobody in Canada plays any sport.

Why: Sports, music, and food travel between places. Each place is still distinct: Canada still has hockey and two official languages, and Mexico still has mariachi.
