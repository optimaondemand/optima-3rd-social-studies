Where Is the Southwest?
📖
Land, Water, and Weather of the Southwest
A walk through the region before you map it

The Southwest sits in the lower-middle part of the United States. The four main states are Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, and Arizona. The southern edge of the Southwest borders the country of Mexico. Texas is so big it stretches all the way to the Gulf of Mexico, but most of the Southwest is far from any ocean.

🏜️ The land. Much of the Southwest is desert: dry land with little rain. Most of it is rock, gravel, shrubs, and cactus, not giant sand dunes. The Sonoran Desert covers parts of Arizona, and the Chihuahuan Desert spreads across New Mexico and Texas. The region has tall, flat-topped hills called mesas and smaller, narrow ones called buttes. Arizona is home to the famous Grand Canyon, which is over one mile deep and 277 miles long.

🌊 The water. Water is precious in the Southwest because it doesn't rain often. The most important river is the Rio Grande, which forms part of the border between Texas and Mexico. The Colorado River carved out the Grand Canyon over millions of years. People here build dams and canals to save and share water.

☀️ The weather. Summer days in the desert are very hot, sometimes over 100°F. But the desert is not always hot! Dry air cools off fast, so nights can be chilly, and winter nights can freeze. The high mountains of Arizona and New Mexico even get snow. Rain is rare, but when it does rain hard, water can rush down dry creek beds in flash floods.

🌵 The people and the work. The Southwest has been home to Pueblo, Navajo, Hopi, and other Native American nations for hundreds and even thousands of years, and they are still here today. Some Pueblo families still live in very old villages with thick walls of adobe (mud and straw bricks) and stone, like Taos Pueblo and Acoma Pueblo. The Navajo Nation is the largest reservation in the United States. The Southwest is also home to many Mexican-American families whose families have lived in this region for generations. Work in the region includes cattle ranching in Texas, oil and gas drilling, growing cotton, and welcoming visitors to the Grand Canyon and other parks. The Southwest also has some of the biggest cities in the country, like Houston, Phoenix, San Antonio, and Dallas.

⏳

Then & Now: Long ago, Pueblo families built adobe homes from mud and straw because that was what the desert provided. Those thick walls kept rooms cool in the daytime and warm at night. Today, builders in the Southwest still use the same idea, sometimes with new materials. What stayed the same about why people in the desert build with thick walls?

🤔

Pause & Think: If you visited the Southwest, would you hike the Grand Canyon, walk through an adobe pueblo village, or see a rodeo in Texas? Why?

The Southwest is a region of deserts, canyons, mesas, and big skies. Its four main states (Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, and Arizona) share hot, dry weather and a long border with Mexico. From the Grand Canyon to Pueblo villages and Mexican-American communities, the Southwest is shaped by its dry land, its precious water, and the many peoples who have called it home for a very long time, and still do today.
