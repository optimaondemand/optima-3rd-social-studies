Where Is the Midwest?
📖
Land, Water, and Climate of the Midwest
A walk through the Heartland before you map it

The Midwest sits in the middle of the country. It includes 12 states: Ohio, Michigan, Indiana, Illinois, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Iowa, Missouri, North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska, and Kansas.

🌾 The land. The Midwest is famous for its flat, wide interior plains — sometimes called the Great Plains farther west. This flatness is what makes the region perfect for farming. The land rolls gently in some places (Ohio, southern Illinois) and is almost entirely flat in others (Kansas, North Dakota).

🌊 The water. The Great Lakes — Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie, and Ontario — sit on the northern edge of the Midwest. These five enormous lakes hold about 21% of the world's surface fresh water. The Mississippi River flows through the middle of the region from Minnesota south toward the Gulf. The Missouri River crosses the western plains.

❄️ The climate. The Midwest has dramatic seasons. Winters are harsh — very cold and snowy in the northern states. Summers are hot and humid, perfect for growing corn and soybeans. Tornadoes are common across the central plains in spring.

🌽 The resources. The Midwest is called the "Breadbasket of America" because it grows enormous amounts of corn, soybeans, wheat, and other grains. Iowa and Illinois alone grow more corn than most countries in the world. The region also has auto manufacturing (Michigan), steel, and important river and lake shipping routes. Chicago (Illinois) is the largest city in the Midwest and a major transportation hub. Detroit (Michigan) is known for the automobile industry.

🔗

Cause → Choice → Consequence:

  • Cause: The Midwest’s flat land has rich, dark soil from thousands of years of prairie grass decomposing.
  • Choice: Settlers plowed the prairie and planted crops on an enormous scale.
  • Consequence: The Midwest became the main food-growing region of the country, earning the name “Breadbasket of America.”

The Big Picture
🏷️ Exhibit Label

The Midwest is the wide, flat heartland of the United States. Its rich soil, the Great Lakes, and the Mississippi River make it one of the greatest farming regions in the world. From the cornfields of Iowa to the auto factories of Michigan, the Midwest shapes how Americans eat, travel, and work.

🦉
🧭 Opti says…
Here’s something wild: Illinois and Iowa grow enormous amounts of corn — but most of it is NOT sweet corn you’d eat on the cob. Most Midwest corn is “field corn,” used for animal feed, ethanol fuel, and making corn syrup. Your bag of chips, your soda, and the gas in a car might all have Midwest corn in them!
