🏛️ Virtue Spotlight — Courtesy
When we study regions, we study real places where real people live. Courtesy means we talk about every region — the ones we've been to and the ones we haven't — with the same respect and curiosity. No region is "boring" or "better." Every alcove in this gallery deserves equal attention.

What Is a Region?
Five alcoves, one country

Geographers divide the country into regions because areas that share the same land, climate, resources, or culture have something important in common. The United States has five major geographic regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, and West.

🏙️ Northeast
Upper-right corner of the U.S. along the Atlantic Ocean. Known for older cities, fishing and lobster, New England forests, and the Appalachian Mountains along its western edge.
🌴 Southeast
Lower-right corner, bordering both the Gulf of Mexico and Atlantic Ocean. Warm climate, the Everglades, cotton and citrus farming, and the southern Appalachians.
🌽 Midwest
The wide interior heartland. Flat plains, Great Lakes, and the Mississippi River make it the country's greatest farming region — the "Breadbasket of America."
🌵 Southwest
Desert landscapes, canyons, the Rio Grande, and oil country. Dry, sunny, and home to some of the most dramatic scenery in the country.
🏔️ West
Pacific coastline, the Rocky Mountains, and the most diverse climates of any region — from rainforests in Washington to deserts in Nevada.
⏳

Then & Now: Long ago, travelers crossing the United States quickly noticed that one area felt completely different from another — the flat, snowy plains felt nothing like dry, rocky canyons. Today we can fly across the whole country in a few hours, but geographers still study those same natural differences to understand the land. What stayed the same about why knowing a region matters?

🔎 Discovery Fact
The United States has 50 states, but geographers typically group them into just 5 regions. Different geographers sometimes draw the boundaries differently — but the five-region system is the most commonly taught in schools!

Geographers divide the United States into five regions — Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, and West. Each region has its own land, climate, resources, and culture. Studying regions helps us understand how the United States is both one country and many different places.
