🏛️ Virtue Spotlight — Honesty
When the council asks a hard question, honesty means we follow the evidence even if our answer is complicated. We don't pick the answer we think sounds good — we pick the one the evidence supports. An honest curator admits when the evidence points in multiple directions.

The Council Question: Fresh Water
💧
Water Evidence Across the Five Regions
Review the evidence before advising the council

The council question is on the table: Which U.S. region has the greatest need for a reliable source of fresh water? Before advising the council, review the key evidence about water and climate across the regions.

🌊 Northeast
Atlantic coastline, many rivers and lakes, adequate rainfall. Generally water-rich. No major shortage.
🌴 Southeast
Gulf and Atlantic coasts, good rainfall in most areas. Some coastal regions face saltwater intrusion. Generally water-rich.
🌽 Midwest
Great Lakes (21% of world's surface fresh water), Mississippi River, adequate rainfall in the east. Western Plains have less rain.
☀️ Southwest — DRIEST REGION
The Sonoran and Chihuahuan Deserts get very little rain. The Colorado River and Rio Grande are the main water sources — and both are under stress. Cities like Phoenix and Las Vegas depend on these rivers.
🌲 West
Mixed. Pacific Northwest gets abundant rain. California's Central Valley depends on irrigation from snowmelt. Droughts have become more frequent and severe.

Council Vote: Two Perspectives — You Decide
⚖️
Two Perspectives — You Decide
Read both sides, then cast your council vote
Perspective A — The Southwest
"The Southwest needs fresh water most. It is the driest region. It depends almost entirely on the Colorado River, which is already over-tapped. Phoenix and Las Vegas are major cities in the desert — and they keep growing."
Perspective B — The West (California)
"The West — especially California — might need fresh water equally as much. California grows food for the entire country, and when droughts hit, the water runs out — affecting farmers and everyone who eats."
Explain your choice: I chose ____ because ____.

Then & Now: Long ago, Native peoples of the Southwest built sophisticated canal systems to bring water from rivers to their desert farms — a practice called irrigation. Today the same principle applies: cities, farms, and industries in the Southwest all depend on canals, dams, and pipelines to move water from where it falls to where people need it. What the Evidence Shows
🔎
What the Evidence Shows
The council weighs in

Both the Southwest and parts of the West face real water challenges. Most water experts agree that the Southwest — especially the Colorado River Basin — faces the most urgent water needs. But this is a question where careful, honest evidence matters. The regions you studied weren't just for the museum exhibit — they're real places where real decisions happen.

🏷️ Exhibit Label

The five regions of the United States have very different relationships with water. Some regions — like the Northeast and Midwest — have abundant fresh water. Others — like the Southwest — face real shortages. Geographers and citizens use maps, climate data, and regional evidence to ask: "Who has water? Who needs it? How do we share it?"
