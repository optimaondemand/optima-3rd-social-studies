🏛️ Virtue Spotlight: Courtesy
A respectful curator never laughs at someone else's money. The pictures, the words, and even the colors on a Canadian dollar or a Mexican peso were chosen carefully by people who care about their country. Today, notice how a courteous curator describes each currency the way its own people would describe it.

Four Neighbors, Four Currencies
🎬
Teacher recording notes: Walk through the four currencies one at a time. United States: the U.S. dollar; bills show presidents and other famous Americans; coins for cents. Canada: the Canadian dollar; plastic bills in many colors; the one-dollar coin is the "loonie" because it shows a bird called a loon, and the two-dollar coin is the "toonie." Mexico: the Mexican peso; the word means "weight" in Spanish; Mexico uses the $ sign too. Caribbean: many currencies. Compare Like a Curator

Four Currencies of North America and the Caribbean
Same four rules, different countries

A country's currency is the kind of money it uses. In a store in the United States, you pay with U.S. dollars. In Canada, just across the northern border, the dollars look different. In Mexico, to the south, the money has another name. And the islands of the Caribbean have their own currencies too.

The United States uses the U.S. dollar. The bills show presidents and other famous Americans. Coins come in cents: pennies, nickels, dimes, and quarters.

In Canada, people use the Canadian dollar. The bills are made of thin plastic in bright colors. The one-dollar coin is nicknamed the "loonie" because it shows a water bird called a loon. The two-dollar coin is the "toonie."

In Mexico, the currency is the Mexican peso. The word peso means "weight" in Spanish, from long ago, when a coin was worth what its silver weighed.

The Caribbean has many countries. Jamaica uses the Jamaican dollar. Cuba uses the Cuban peso. Puerto Rico belongs to the United States, so it uses the U.S. dollar. Eight islands share one currency, the Eastern Caribbean dollar.

Three of these are called "dollars," but they are different money with different values. Every one of them follows the four rules, and the people who use it trust it.
