Learn the Story
📖
Why Goods Travel
Region, resource, route: the same scarcity story on a bigger map

Remember the coastal town and the valley farm? One had plenty of fish but no grain. The other had plenty of grain but no fish. So they traded. The same thing happens between whole regions, just on a much bigger map.

Each region of North America and the Caribbean has its own resources, the things the land or water gives that people use. Canada has huge forests and oil, so it sends timber and fuel south. The U.S. prairies grow wheat and corn for the whole country and beyond. Florida grows oranges that ship out to grocery stores far from any orange tree. Mexico grows avocados and peppers and has silver in its mountains. The Caribbean islands grow bananas, sugarcane, and coffee in warm weather that no northern region has.

A trade route is the path goods travel from where they come from to where they are needed. A trade route map shows those paths as arrows. Long ago the arrows were sailing ships, steamships, and wagons. Today they are trucks, trains, planes, and cargo ships. The reason for the arrows hasn't changed: one region has scarcity of something, and another region has plenty.

1. What is a trade route?
AThe path goods travel from a region with plenty to a region where they are scarce.
BA line that shows where two countries share a border.

Yes! A trade route is the path goods take from where they are plentiful to where they are needed.

2. Why do people in Canada buy oranges from Florida instead of growing their own?
ABecause Canadians don't like oranges.
BBecause oranges start at the grocery store, so nobody needs to grow them.
CBecause orange trees can't live through Canada's cold winters. That is scarcity.
Exactly! Orange trees need warm weather. Canada is too cold, and Florida has plenty.
Think about the farm in the Lobby. What happened to the orange tree in January?
3. Why does honesty matter on a trade route map?
ABecause honest maps are always more colorful.
BIf a map shows a resource in the wrong place, traders make bad choices and waste time and money.
CBecause honesty makes the paper stronger.
Right! A trade map is evidence. If it isn't honest, traders go where the resource isn't.
Think about today's virtue. What goes wrong for a trader who follows a wrong map?

Scarcity: What was missing in the region buying it?
